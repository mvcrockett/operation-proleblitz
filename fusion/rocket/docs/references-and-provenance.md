## External technical references (for context only)

1. NASA. [Space Nuclear Propulsion: nuclear thermal vs nuclear electric](https://www.nasa.gov/space-technology-mission-directorate/tdm/space-nuclear-propulsion/). Describes separate heat-to-electric-to-thruster energy path and the low-thrust nature of electric propulsion. This NASA page discusses fission; using it for *fusion-electric coupling* is an analogy, not NASA validation of Crockett fusion.
2. NASA. [Solar Electric Propulsion](https://www.nasa.gov/space-technology-mission-directorate/tdm/solar-electric-propulsion/). Electric propellant acceleration and high propellant efficiency.
3. NASA. [Advanced Lightweight Heat Rejection Radiators for Space Nuclear Power Systems](https://techport.nasa.gov/projects/118274). Spacecraft radiators are large/mass-intensive and require a dedicated design.
4. NASA NTRS. [Thermal Systems Engineering for Nuclear Electric Propulsion High Temperature Radiators](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20250007547). Development context for radiator engineering.
5. Chisholm et al. [Design and initial results from the Junior Levitated Dipole Experiment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.17691). Levitated-dipole experimental context; **does not validate the Crockett Reactor's combined performance projections**.
6. NASA. [In-Space Propulsion state of the art](https://www.nasa.gov/smallsat-institute/sst-soa/in-space_propulsion/). Scope and regimes for electric thruster technologies.
