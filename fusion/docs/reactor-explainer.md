## Why Fusion Matters

### The Dream: A Star in a Bottle

Every second, the sun fuses hydrogen into helium, and turns a sliver of mass into pure energy.

Fusion power would mean electricity with no carbon emissions, no meltdown risk, and fuel extracted from ordinary water.

The fuel supply is effectively limitless, millions of years' worth in the oceans.

**So why don't we have it yet?**

## The Problem

**"Fusion is 30 years away, and always will be."**

That joke is 70 years old. Three problems have defeated every reactor design so far, all at once.

**This reactor doesn't patch those problems.**

**It removes them. Architecturally.**

## Why Fusion Keeps Failing

### Bottleneck 1: The Soggy Firewood Problem

Today's reactors feed fuel in at the plasma's edge, like trying to light wet firewood from the outside.

The fuel cools before it ever reaches the hot core. Most of the heating energy is wasted.

Result: ignition demands enormous external heaters, and efficiency collapses.

### Bottleneck 2: The Leaky Bottle Problem

Feeding fuel at the edge stirs up turbulence in the plasma, like shaking a bottle you're trying to keep still.

Turbulence breaks magnetic confinement. The plasma wobbles, cools, and the reaction dies.

Result: reactions can't be sustained long enough to matter.

### Bottleneck 3: The Crumbling Cage Problem

Fusion releases neutrons that slam into the reactor's solid walls, making the metal brittle over time.

Every 5–10 years, the wall must be replaced, at a cost of hundreds of millions of dollars.

Result: even a working reactor can never run indefinitely, and can never be cheap.

## The Big Idea

### Four Layers, One Closed Loop

1. Outer vacuum chamber
2. FLiBe liquid blanket
3. Levitated magnetic bottle
4. Plasma core

*Each layer fixes one bottleneck. Together, they close the loop.*

## Solution 1 · Fixes the Soggy Firewood

### Light the Fire From the Center

Instead of puffing fuel at the edge, tiny layered pellets are fired at up to 5 km/s straight into the plasma core.

A heat-resistant coating vaporizes at exactly the right depth, releasing fuel where it's hottest.

**The burn starts in the middle and spreads outward: inside-out ignition.**

Like lighting a campfire from the center instead of waving a match at the outside.

## Solution 2 · Fixes the Leaky Bottle

### Refuel Without Shaking the Bottle

Because fuel arrives at the core, not the edge, the plasma stays calm and peaked where it matters.

No edge turbulence. No wobble. Magnetic confinement holds rock-solid while refueling continues.

**A stable plasma is a plasma that keeps burning.**

## Solution 3 · Fixes the Crumbling Cage

### The Bottle That Never Breaks

The magnetic bottle holding the plasma doesn't touch anything, it floats, levitated by a second magnetic field.

Neutrons fly straight through the vacuum gap to a distant blanket. No wall in their path means no wall to destroy.

Levitated-dipole confinement has already been demonstrated in laboratory experiments.

**No embrittlement. No replacement cycles. Indefinite runtime.**

[Sweep note: the wall is genuinely out of the neutron path, but the levitated magnet itself is not. It sits between the plasma and the gap and takes the full neutron flux first, so magnet embrittlement is an open problem for this design, not a solved one. See docs/full-design-document.md, Section 4.3, and OPEN-QUESTIONS.md.]

## The Engine Room

### The Blanket That Cooks and Breeds

A shell of molten salt (FLiBe) circles the core at 500–700°C, catching neutron energy as heat.

The same salt breeds tritium fuel from lithium, making about twice the fuel the reactor consumes.

[Sweep 1 note: the breeding ratio of about 2 is an unverified design target. No supporting calculation appears in the corpus, and published blanket studies target about 1.05 to 1.15. See OPEN-QUESTIONS.md.]

**Heat drives a turbine. Fresh fuel feeds new pellets. The loop closes.**

After startup, the reactor makes its own fuel, forever.

## Safety

### Safe by Physics, Not by Procedure

Fusion has no chain reaction. If anything goes wrong, the plasma cools and the reaction simply stops.

If power is lost, the levitated bottle settles gently to the chamber floor. No meltdown is physically possible.

Total on-site fuel inventory: 1–2 kg of tritium, continuously recycled, not tons of fuel rods.

**Waste decays to background levels in decades, not millennia.**

[Sweep 1 note: three claims on this slide are flagged in FACT-CHECK-LOG.md. A settling superconducting magnet needs a drop and quench analysis before it can be called gentle. One to two kilograms of tritium is a large, heavily regulated inventory, not a small one, though continuous recycling is a real design strength. Waste timelines depend on material choices not yet specified. See OPEN-QUESTIONS.md.]

## Economics

### Cheaper Than Gas, Cleaner Than Everything

Projected electricity cost: $28–$34 per MWh, below new natural gas ($45–60) and far below new fission ($80–120).

Projected build cost 54–75% lower per kW than traditional tokamak designs.

**Fuel cost after startup: ~$0. The reactor breeds its own.**

60-year plant life with no wall replacement. Capacity factor ~92%.

All figures are projections from the design model, not guarantees.

[Sweep 1 note: Lazard's LCOE+ estimates (2025 edition) put new combined cycle gas at about $48 to $109 per MWh and new nuclear fission at about $141 to $220 per MWh, so the comparison ranges on this slide sit below the published new build ranges. The build cost advantage is stated per kW on a thermal basis in the design document. See FACT-CHECK-LOG.md.]
