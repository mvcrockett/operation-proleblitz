## 4. Technology Package 1: The Crockett Reactor, a Wall-Less Fusion Architecture

### 4.1 The problem

Conventional magnetic fusion is bottlenecked on three fronts at once: fuel introduced at the plasma edge wastes heating energy reaching the core; edge fueling drives turbulence that degrades confinement; and neutron bombardment embrittles solid first walls, forcing costly replacement cycles that make indefinite operation impossible.

### 4.2 The proposed solution

A four-layer nested architecture: (1) an outer vacuum chamber; (2) a circulating molten-salt (FLiBe) blanket for heat extraction and tritium breeding, positioned 1–2 meters from the core; (3) a high-temperature superconducting magnetic bottle magnetically levitated inside the chamber with no solid supports in the neutron path; and (4) a plasma core fueled by multi-layered ablative pellets injected at high velocity directly to the magnetic axis, where a timing-engineered coating vaporizes and releases deuterium–tritium fuel at peak temperature and pressure, inside-out ignition. Because neutrons cross a 0.5–1 meter vacuum gap to reach the distant blanket, no structural wall sits in the neutron flux: no embrittlement, no replacement cycles.

### 4.3 Preliminary projections

Inventor's estimates, April 2026 design document. Independent validation required.

| Parameter | Projected Value |
| --- | --- |
| Projected energy gain (Q) | Greater than 10 |
| Tritium breeding ratio | Approximately 2.01 (self-sustaining after initial loading) |
| Pilot plant thermal output | 500 MWth |
| Net electrical output | Approximately 186 MWe |
| Thermal-to-electric efficiency | Approximately 47% (supercritical CO2 Brayton cycle) |
| Projected levelized cost of electricity | $28–34/MWh |
| Projected capital cost | Approximately $3,700/kW, versus $8,000–$15,000/kW for conventional tokamak benchmarks |
| Plant lifetime | 60 years (no first-wall replacement) |
| Runtime | Indefinite (zero wall embrittlement by design) |
