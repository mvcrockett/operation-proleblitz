## The Problem

Every major fusion architecture still fails on three fronts: ignition inefficiency ("soggy firewood"), plasma instability ("leaky bottle"), and neutron embrittlement of solid reactor walls ("crumbling cage"). Current approaches patch these problems individually. The Crockett Reactor eliminates all three architecturally.

## The Solution

A three-pillar wall-less fusion architecture:

**I. Core Ignition System** — Multi-layered, time-delayed ablative fuel pellets injected at high velocity directly into the plasma core. The coating vaporizes en route, triggering inside-out ignition at peak temperature and pressure. Result: dramatically higher Q-value with minimal external heating.

**II. Stability Advantage** — Central surgical-strike refueling replaces edge gas puffing, minimizing turbulence and maintaining rock-solid magnetic confinement for sustained shots.

**III. Wall-Less Levitated Core** — The primary magnetic confinement bottle is magnetically levitated inside a larger vacuum chamber by a second external field. Neutrons travel harmlessly through the vacuum gap and are captured by a distant spherical FLiBe liquid blanket for heat extraction and tritium breeding. No solid first wall. No neutron embrittlement. No replacement cycles. Indefinite runtime. [Sweep note: the wall is out of the neutron path, but the levitated magnet itself is not. It takes the full neutron flux first, so magnet embrittlement is an open problem for this design, not a solved one. See OPEN-QUESTIONS.md.]

## 2026 Validation

OpenStar Technologies achieved fully levitated plasma confinement in its Junior prototype in February 2026, floating a roughly 0.5-tonne (560 kg) HTS magnet inside a 5.2-meter vacuum chamber with no solid supports. Levitated dipole confinement was first demonstrated by the LDX experiment at MIT and the RT-1 device at the University of Tokyo, and OpenStar's published paper credits both. Junior is the first commercial levitated dipole. This is Part II of the Crockett Reactor, the levitated core, in real hardware at prototype scale. The levitation physics works. We scale it and add the distant spherical blanket geometry. [Corrected in Sweep 1, see FACT-CHECK-LOG.md. The original text called this the world's first levitated dipole plasma confinement and numbered the levitated core as Part III.]

- CAPEX: ~$3,700/kW vs. $8,000–$15,000/kW for traditional tokamak designs
