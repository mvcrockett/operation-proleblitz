# Why I'm Giving Away the Secret to Nuclear Fusion

Source: "Why I'm giving away the secret to nuclear fusion," a Google Doc essay by Mary-Victoria Crockett, October 23, 2025, marked "(revised)" at the end. This is the earliest document in the corpus and the founding statement of the open source decision. Converted to markdown with wording preserved and paragraph breaks cleaned only.

---

Have you ever come up with a good idea? What about ten? What about twenty? I’m good at coming up with good ideas. But as an inventor, getting rid of them is the real trouble. 



Last year, I came up with what I would consider a really good idea. I solved a few bottleneck problems in nuclear fusion. But like my other ideas, I hit a bit of a snag. 



How the hell was I supposed to scale up nuclear fusion?



A lot of inventors are cagey when it comes to their big breakthrough. They guard it fiercely like a dragon protecting it’s precious gold. They guard it so fiercely, in fact, that most people never even get to see it. 



I didn’t want my invention to wither on a shelf. I knew that what I was sitting on could help too many people. 



So I decided to do something a bit extreme. 



I decided that, today, I would give my invention away. That I would, hopefully with some talented people who want to join me, start a non-profit. 



Are you kidding? I’m sure most people want to say. You’re just giving away the secret to nuclear fusion?



Yes. Yes, I am. Because as one of the only prolific female inventors on this earth, I know something most people never come to know intimately: the value of a single life. 



In our lifetimes, millions if not billions could be displaced from their homes due to widespread, catastrophic climate change. And when crop shortages come, when droughts come, how will we desalinate water without viable limitless energy?



The answer is we won’t. Not unless I give it away. 



One thing that I’ve learned from being a prolific inventor is that you can manufacture all the cheap, plastic crap you want. But the only thing that matters –the only thing that has ever mattered, is the value of a single life. 



A life without wealth, a life without privilege –that is our value and our worth as a species. The value that we place on one, single life. There is no such thing as an ordinary human. 



So the only question you probably have left is how does it work?



I'm going to start my answer by making you aware of some key problems that exist today in nuclear fusion.

Right now, we have three main bottlenecks preventing us from achieving stable, commercial fusion energy. I call them: The Soggy Firewood Problem, The Leaky Bottle Problem, and The Crumbling Cage Problem.

The Soggy Firewood Problem is about ignition. Current reactors try to heat the entire plasma volume from the outside in. It's like trying to start a campfire by warming the air around it instead of lighting the kindling at the center. It takes enormous amounts of energy, and most of the time, you never reach the temperatures needed for sustained fusion.

The Leaky Bottle Problem is about stability. Plasma is incredibly difficult to contain. It's hotter than the sun, and even our strongest magnetic fields struggle to hold it. Current methods involve puffing hydrogen gas at the edges, which creates turbulence. The plasma churns, destabilizes, and the whole reaction collapses before you can extract useful energy.

The Crumbling Cage Problem is about materials. When fusion happens, it releases high-energy neutrons that bombard the reactor walls. Over time, those neutrons make the materials brittle. They crack. They fail. And replacing reactor walls is expensive and time-consuming. It's like trying to build a cage that won't melt, crack, or crumble when you put the sun inside it.

These three problems have stumped researchers for decades. Billions of dollars have been spent trying to solve them individually.

But what if we could solve all three at once?

Here's what I'm proposing—what I call the Crockett Reactor.

First: Core Ignition. Instead of heating plasma from the outside, we inject specially designed fuel pellets directly into the reactor's core. These pellets have a heat-resistant coating that vaporizes as they travel through the plasma, exposing fresh, dense fuel exactly where it's hottest and most compressed. This triggers ignition from the inside out—like lighting kindling at the heart of the fire instead of trying to warm wet logs. It requires dramatically less external energy and produces a self-sustaining reaction.

Second: Surgical Stability. By refueling at the core instead of puffing gas at the edges, we eliminate the turbulence that causes plasma instability. It's a surgical strike instead of a blunt force approach. The magnetic field stays intact. The plasma stays stable. And a stable plasma means longer confinement, which means more energy output.

Third: The Wall-less Core. Here's the radical part. The ultimate solution to materials failure is to remove the material from the point of failure. Instead of building walls that will inevitably crack under neutron bombardment, we suspend the fusion core in mid-air using magnetic levitation. The plasma floats inside a vacuum chamber, held in place by an outer magnetic field. The neutrons pass harmlessly through the vacuum and are absorbed by a distant liquid blanket—molten salt or lithium—that can be continuously circulated for heat exchange and tritium breeding. No solid walls. No neutron embrittlement. No structural failure. The reactor can run indefinitely.

This is it. A reactor with no walls to fail, a plasma that stays stable, and an ignition method that actually works.

Now, I know what you're thinking. If this works, why are you giving it away?

Because I believe in the value of a single life more than I believe in profit. Because climate change is coming faster than any one company or country can handle alone. Because the only way humanity survives the next century is if we stop hoarding solutions and start sharing them.

I'm not a billionaire. I'm not a physicist with a lab and a research team. I'm just someone who saw the world burning around me and found myself with a fire extinguisher. 

So today, I'm releasing this design as open source. Anyone can build on it. Anyone can test it. Anyone can improve it. And anyone can join me in a non-profit to build it. Because the abundance of free energy should belong to humanity.

So if you're a researcher, an engineer, a student, or just someone who doesn’t want to watch the world burn, I ask that you join me. Let's build this together. Let's make fusion real. Not for profit. Not for patents. But because it's the right thing to do.

Thank you.
