# Open Questions

This file is the honest core of the Crockett Reactor release. Every claim in the corpus that is novel, untested, internally unresolved, or missing its supporting work is listed here in plain language, with the test or calculation that would settle it.

Nothing here is a rhetorical question. Each item is a real piece of work a collaborator could pick up. If you settle one, in either direction, the answer belongs in FACT-CHECK-LOG.md and the correction belongs in the documents, credited to you.

Status words used below: HYPOTHESIS means the author's novel claim, stated clearly and awaiting a test. UNVERIFIED means a figure or fact asserted in the corpus with no source or calculation behind it that the sweeps could find. UNRESOLVED means the corpus contradicts itself or leaves a gap no editor can close.

## Fuel cycle

1. **The breeding ratio of about 2.01. HYPOTHESIS, and the biggest open number in the design.** Every fusion event produces exactly one neutron. That neutron can breed at most one tritium atom in lithium 6, unless beryllium multiplication and the lithium 7 reaction add more, and leakage and structural capture subtract. Published blanket studies target about 1.05 to 1.15. No calculation supporting 2.01 exists in the corpus. What would settle it: a neutronics model of the stated blanket (20 to 40 cm FLiBe, stated enrichment, beryllium multiplier) in a standard tool such as OpenMC, published with its inputs.
2. **Fuel self-sufficiency and near zero fuel cost. UNRESOLVED, because they depend on item 1.** If the real breeding ratio lands near published values, the reactor still breeds its own fuel with a small surplus, but the economics change. If it lands below 1, the design needs an external tritium supply that barely exists.
3. **Lithium 6 enrichment: 20 percent or 20 to 50 percent. UNRESOLVED.** Section 5.1 of the design document models 20 percent. The patent claim section specifies 20 to 50 percent. The author needs to pick, or a neutronics collaborator needs to show which range the breeding claim requires.
4. **Tritium extraction at over 95 percent per pass. UNVERIFIED.** Helium sparging and palladium membranes are real techniques. The specific efficiency figure has no source in the corpus. What would settle it: a citation from the molten salt or fusion fuel cycle literature, or test data.
5. **A kilogram scale tritium inventory described as minimal. Framing corrected, quantity still a design question.** One to two kilograms is large by regulatory standards. Whether the continuous extraction loop can actually hold the inventory at the low end while feeding pellet production is a fuel cycle design calculation nobody has done yet.

## Magnets and levitation

6. **Neutron damage to the levitated magnet itself. HYPOTHESIS with a known hard problem attached.** The design removes the solid first wall, and the wall genuinely leaves the neutron path. The levitated HTS magnet does not. It sits between the plasma and the vacuum gap and takes the full 14.1 MeV flux first. Superconductor performance and magnet insulation degrade under neutron irradiation. The indefinite runtime claim stands or falls here. What would settle it: a fluence and displacement damage estimate for the magnet at the stated geometry and power, plus a shielding or replacement strategy if the numbers are bad.
7. **Levitation stability under continuous pellet injection. HYPOTHESIS.** Static magnetic levitation is not stable in every direction without feedback, and every working levitated dipole uses active control. The design's microsecond feedback loop is therefore load-bearing, and pellets arriving at kilometers per second will perturb both the plasma and, through it, the bottle. What would settle it: a control simulation with realistic sensor noise, actuator limits, and injection perturbations, or a bench experiment.
8. **The levitation force law and sizing. UNRESOLVED.** Sweep 1 corrected the falloff from r cubed to r to the fourth. The exact force expression for this two-magnet geometry, the magnet masses, the field strengths, and the stable operating gap have never been derived or sized in the corpus. What would settle it: a derivation and a sizing table.
9. **Scaling from Junior class prototypes to a 500 MWth plant. UNVERIFIED.** Demonstrated levitated dipoles are meter scale research devices with no blanket, no breeding, no power conversion, and no high speed pellet injection. Every plant scale subsystem in this design is undemonstrated. This is not an objection, it is the work list.

## Pellets and ignition

10. **How the pellet crosses the blanket and the magnet. UNRESOLVED, and possibly the sharpest mechanical gap in the design.** Injectors mount on the chamber wall. The blanket shell and the levitated magnet structure both sit between the wall and the magnetic axis. No document explains how a pellet crosses either, or what the crossing does to the salt layer, the field, or the pellet. Ports, gaps in the blanket, and timing windows are all possible answers. None is in the corpus.
11. **Injection at 1 to 5 km/s with the pellet intact. HYPOTHESIS.** Operating pellet injectors on fusion machines fire at hundreds of meters per second. Kilometer per second speeds are railgun territory, and a layered cryogenic pellet surviving acceleration and then a plasma edge crossing at those speeds is undemonstrated. What would settle it: injector literature at these speeds, or a survivability calculation for the stated pellet.
12. **Ablation timing to the magnetic axis. HYPOTHESIS.** The timing equation in the design document is dimensionally consistent, but it uses a single heat flux value for a path along which the flux varies by orders of magnitude, and the stated flux of about GW per square meter in the core has no source. Real ablation modeling is path dependent. What would settle it: an ablation simulation along a realistic trajectory, or a worked sensitivity analysis showing how much timing error the design tolerates.
13. **Inside-out ignition producing Q greater than 10. HYPOTHESIS, the central physics claim of the design.** No magnetic confinement device has exceeded Q of 0.67 (JET, 1997). Core fueling is a real and studied technique, and levitated dipoles are known for peaked pressure profiles, which is genuinely suggestive. But the jump from those facts to Q over 10 needs a burn calculation. What would settle it: a 0D or 1D power balance and transport model for the stated geometry, with assumptions published.
14. **Central fueling driving the tearing-mode index negative. HYPOTHESIS.** Stated in the design document as a result, supported by no calculation. Dipole geometry stability is its own subject and the published LDX work on peaked profiles is relevant background, but the specific claim needs an MHD stability analysis.
15. **The boron or lithium catalyst layer. UNVERIFIED.** The pellet spec says trace boron or lithium boosts reactivity and Q. No basis is given, and the sweeps found no published basis for boron improving a D-T burn. Either a mechanism and a citation, or the layer comes out of the spec.

## Materials

16. **Graphene-copper surviving in FLiBe at 500 to 700 degrees C. UNVERIFIED.** The corpus quotes specific corrosion rates (10 to 50 microns per year untreated, over 99 percent reduction with the coating, under 0.5 microns per year long term) with no source, and the sweeps found no published data on graphene coated copper in hot FLiBe. Graphene adhesion and stability in fluoride salt at temperature is exactly where a reviewer will push. What would settle it: a literature citation, or a coupon test in hot FLiBe.
17. **Fusion decay heat of 1 to 2 percent of operating power. UNVERIFIED.** The figure depends on the activation of the specific materials chosen, which the corpus does not fix. What would settle it: an activation calculation for a stated materials set.
18. **Waste decaying to background in decades. UNVERIFIED as stated.** Tritium decays with a 12.3 year half-life, and carefully chosen low activation materials can reach clearance in roughly a century. The claim needs a materials list and an activation inventory behind it.

## Geometry

19. **The dimension stack does not close. UNRESOLVED.** Chamber 3 to 5 meters in diameter, blanket 1 to 2 meters from the core and 20 to 40 cm thick, vacuum gap 0.5 to 1 meter, bottle size unstated. At the small end the parts cannot all fit. What would settle it: one dimensioned geometry, radius by radius, that every document and diagram then follows.
20. **A spherical blanket inside a cylindrical chamber. UNRESOLVED.** The corpus describes both shapes in adjacent paragraphs. One geometry has to win, and the breeding and heat calculations depend on which one it is.
21. **The heat balance gap. UNRESOLVED.** The heat loop diagram deposits about 400 MW in the blanket, matching the 80 percent neutron fraction of 500 MWth. The power balance applies the 47 percent efficiency to the full 500 MWth. The path for the remaining roughly 100 MW of alpha energy into the power cycle is never described. Closing this changes gross output by about a fifth if the answer is unfavorable.
22. **FLiBe flow at 5 to 10 m/s. UNVERIFIED as a workable spec.** Hot fluoride salt at high velocity aggravates erosion and corrosion, the very problem Section 5.3 works to solve. The figure needs a justification or a revision.

## Safety and licensing

23. **The dropped magnet. UNRESOLVED.** The corpus says the levitated bottle settles safely if power is lost. A superconducting magnet of this class carries large stored magnetic energy, and a drop can mean impact damage and a quench. No drop or quench analysis exists in the corpus. What would settle it: an engineering analysis of the drop, the quench, and the chamber floor's job in both.
24. **The pH and color-changing plasma-edge sensor. UNEXPLAINED.** It appears in the closed-loop description, the safety table, and patent Claim 3. pH is a liquid chemistry measure, and no document says what this sensor measures at a plasma edge or how. Until the mechanism is stated, the claim built on it cannot be evaluated. This one is the author's to explain or withdraw.
25. **Licensing timeline and the digital twin claim. UNVERIFIED.** The Part 30 framework is real and favorable, and that part of the corpus is correct. The 12 to 18 month approval target and the claim that existing simulation data provides 90 percent of the safety analysis are the author's projections with no regulatory source. The design document also references a digital twin. The underlying modeling notes exist in the author's files and were inventoried for this release but are not part of this drop, and whether any model output supports any figure in the corpus is UNVERIFIED.

## Economics

26. **Every CAPEX line item. UNVERIFIED.** The eight line items sum correctly to 1.855 billion dollars, and that is all the sweeps could establish. None is tied to a quote, a vendor, or a published cost study. The HTS magnet line at 480 million dollars for a levitated plant scale magnet is the item most in need of a basis.
27. **The cost per kW comparison. CORRECTED, and the honest version is an open question.** On the electric basis buyers use, the plant's own figures give about 9,970 dollars per kW, inside the tokamak benchmark range. Whether the architecture can actually beat conventional designs on cost per kW electric is a real question that only a bottom-up cost model can answer.
28. **A 92 percent capacity factor. HYPOTHESIS.** No fusion device has run at power plant capacity factors. The figure follows from the indefinite runtime claim, which follows from open question 6. The chain is only as strong as its first link.
29. **The tokamak benchmark range of 8,000 to 15,000 dollars per kW. UNVERIFIED to a single source.** Plausible against published studies, unpinned in this release. A collaborator with a good citation improves every comparison in the corpus by fixing this one.

## Status and provenance

30. **Patent status. UNKNOWN after September 30, 2026.** The author's DOE proposal of that date states that no patent applications had yet been filed. The deck and design document say patent pending. No filing is documented in the corpus. This release is published under open licenses regardless, see the README, but the record should be accurate about what exists.
31. **The submission history. PARTLY VERIFIED.** The ARPA-E commitment the pitch responds to is real (135 million dollars, April 8, 2026). Whether the pitch or the design document was submitted, received, or answered is not documented in the corpus, and this release claims none of those things.
