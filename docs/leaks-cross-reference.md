## How this file was built

Every fact below comes from the ICIJ Offshore Leaks reporting and database, court records, DOJ and regulator releases, or major outlet investigations. Each section carries its source URLs. Anything single-sourced or contested is marked FLAGGED where it appears. A person is named only where a court document, a settlement, or an ICIJ-grade source documents a financial or legal fact about them. Nobody is named for social proximity alone. Inclusion in a leak database is not evidence of wrongdoing, and ICIJ says so on every database page. Offshore structures have legal uses. The audit question is not whether any one structure broke a law. The audit question is what the system costs the people who cannot use it.

## The four troves at a glance

**Panama Papers (2016).** 11.5 million documents, 2.6 terabytes, from the Panamanian law firm Mossack Fonseca. Records run from 1977 to 2015 and cover more than 214,000 offshore entities in 21 jurisdictions, touching people in more than 200 countries. The files named 140 politicians and public officials, including 12 current or former world leaders, and traced about $2 billion moved by associates of Vladimir Putin through banks and shadow companies. The source, who called himself John Doe, gave the files to Süddeutsche Zeitung, and roughly 400 journalists at more than 100 outlets worked on them through ICIJ. Source: https://www.icij.org/investigations/panama-papers/explore-panama-papers-key-figures/

**Paradise Papers (2017).** 13.4 million files, 1.4 terabytes, from the Bermuda law firm Appleby, the Singapore trust company Asiaciti Trust, and company registries in 19 secrecy jurisdictions. Süddeutsche Zeitung again received the leak and shared it with ICIJ and 95 media partners. Nearly 7 million documents came from Appleby alone, spanning about 50 years. The files covered more than 120 politicians and world leaders and the tax planning of roughly 100 multinationals, including Apple, Nike, and Uber. Source: https://www.icij.org/investigations/paradise-papers/icij-releases-paradise-papers/

**Pandora Papers (2021).** 11.9 million files, 2.9 terabytes, from 14 offshore service providers, worked by more than 600 journalists at 150 outlets. The files named 35 current and former country leaders, more than 330 politicians and public officials in over 90 countries, and more than 130 billionaires. ICIJ counted 956 offshore companies tied to 336 high-level politicians, and more than two-thirds of those companies were set up in the British Virgin Islands. The biggest provider in the trove was Trident Trust, with more than 3.3 million records. The second was Alcogal, a Panamanian firm tied to nearly half the politicians named. Sources: https://www.icij.org/investigations/pandora-papers/global-investigation-tax-havens-offshore/?gclid=Cj0KCQjwwY-LBhD6ARIsACvT72OERpNRbXg95tv7Bce-66R5zZc4kiAhxbVRrl4SFN_q659Vf_9fQocaAlVeEALw_wcB and https://www.occrp.org/en/project/the-pandora-papers/faq-about-the-pandora-papers

**The Epstein files (2019 to present).** Not a leak from one firm. This trove is the public record built by courts and governments: the 2019 SDNY indictment (case 19 Cr. 490), the USVI probate case (ST-19-PB-80), the USVI civil enforcement case against the estate (ST-20-CV-14), the USVI cases against JPMorgan and the estate's banks, regulator consent orders, Senate Finance Committee work, House Oversight releases, and the DOJ document releases made under the Epstein Files Transparency Act in 2025 and 2026. Epstein pleaded guilty in Florida in 2008 to state charges including soliciting a minor. He died in federal custody in August 2019 while awaiting trial on federal sex trafficking charges. Those are court facts, stated here once so the financial sections can stay financial.

## Part 1: The connective tissue between the troves

The four troves look like four stories. Read together, they are one story told four times, about a professional industry that sells secrecy as a product.

**The same firms keep showing up.** Asiaciti Trust is a source firm in two different leaks four years apart. Its files appear in the Paradise Papers in 2017 and again in the Pandora Papers in 2021, where it sits on ICIJ's list of the 14 providers at the heart of the trove. A leak did not end the business. It did not even end the filing system. Source: https://projects.icij.org/investigations/pandora-papers/charts/who-are-the-firms-at-the-heart-of-the-pandora-papers?initialWidth=683&childId=icij-iframe-4&parentTitle=Pandora%20Papers%3A%20An%20offshore%20data%20tsunami%20-%20ICIJ&parentUrl=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.icij.org%2Finvestigations%2Fpandora-papers%2Fabout-pandora-papers-leak-dataset%2F

**The enabler layer is bigger than any one firm.** Mossack Fonseca worked with more than 14,000 banks, law firms, incorporators, and other middlemen who brought it clients. ICIJ's Paradise Papers reporting on Russian lawmaker Andrei Skoch named the same professional class on the other side of the world: Appleby, Baker McKenzie, and PwC all appear in that one story about one man's secret companies, yachts, and villa. The product is not a company. The product is a chain of respectable intermediaries, each holding one piece of paper. Source: https://www.icij.org/investigations/paradise-papers/how-russias-richest-lawmaker-used-his-partners-secret-companies-to-own-two-yachts-and-a-seychelles-villa/

**The same families appear in trove after trove.** The circle of Ilham Aliyev, the president of Azerbaijan, appears in the Panama Papers through Mossack Fonseca companies set up for his children, and again in the Pandora Papers through Trident Trust: 84 British Virgin Islands companies and a UK property empire reported at roughly $700 million (ICIJ and OCCRP reporting. Another ICIJ account puts the property figure at over half a billion dollars, so treat the exact total as a range). ICIJ reported that some of those companies received money from the Russian Laundromat and the Troika Laundromat, two of the largest money-moving schemes ever documented. The singer Shakira appears in the Paradise Papers with a Malta company holding more than $30 million in music rights, and in the Pandora Papers with applications for three offshore companies in 2019, which her representatives said were filed to dissolve existing ones. Spain prosecuted her over 2012 to 2014 taxes and she settled in November 2023 with a €7.3 million fine. Repeat appearance does not prove repeat wrongdoing. It proves the customer base is stable. Sources: https://www.icij.org/investigations/pandora-papers/global-investigation-tax-havens-offshore/?gclid=Cj0KCQjwwY-LBhD6ARIsACvT72OERpNRbXg95tv7Bce-66R5zZc4kiAhxbVRrl4SFN_q659Vf_9fQocaAlVeEALw_wcB and https://www.occrp.org/en/project/the-pandora-papers/faq-about-the-pandora-papers

**The same structures do the same jobs.** Mossack Fonseca's stock tools were bearer shares, which belong to whoever physically holds the paper, and nominee directors, who lend their names so the real owner stays off the record. The Pandora-era tool is the perpetual dynasty trust, mostly American (Part 2). The Laundromat tool was the layered shell chain, dozens of companies passing money in circles until the trail went cold. The names change by decade. The function does not: separate the asset from the owner on paper, and put a border, a trustee, or a nominee between the money and any questions.

**Epstein is the bridge between the leak troves and the court troves.** He is not a Panama Papers or Pandora Papers figure. He is a Paradise Papers figure. ICIJ's Offshore Leaks Database, built from the Appleby files, lists Jeffrey E. Epstein as a director of Liquid Funding, Ltd. from November 9, 2001 to March 30, 2007, and as its chairman from November 9, 2001 to March 19, 2007. Liquid Funding was incorporated in Bermuda on October 19, 2000, with Appleby as its agent. The address ICIJ has on file for Epstein is 6100 Red Hook Quarter, Suite B3, St. Thomas, US Virgin Islands, the same address his USVI companies used across the court record. His banks tie the troves together too. JPMorgan, his bank from 1998 to 2013, had absorbed Bear Stearns in 2008, and Bear Stearns held about 40 percent of Liquid Funding (secondary accounts credit Bear Stearns with creating it, FLAGGED in Part 5). Deutsche Bank, his bank from 2013 to 2018, appears across the leak era as a laundromat-era institution in its own right (its NYDFS penalty, covered in Part 4, also covered the Danske Bank Estonia and FBME matters). Sources: https://offshoreleaks.icij.org/nodes/82004676 and https://offshoreleaks.icij.org/nodes/80063035

**What the leaks actually changed.** Governments have recovered more than $1.36 billion in taxes and fines from Panama Papers follow-up work, per ICIJ's running count. That is real money and it is also a rounding error against the annual flow figures in Part 3. Mossack Fonseca closed in 2018. In June 2024 a Panamanian judge acquitted all 28 former firm employees on trial, including co-founder Jürgen Mossack, over chain-of-custody problems with the evidence. Co-founder Ramón Fonseca had died in May 2024 before the trial concluded, and the case against him was dismissed. The firm died. The industry did not. Source: https://www.icij.org/investigations/panama-papers/explore-panama-papers-key-figures/

## Part 2: The American angle

The leak troves were first read in the US as a foreign scandal. The Pandora Papers ended that reading. By 2021 the United States was not just a customer of the secrecy industry. It was one of its biggest suppliers.

**South Dakota is a top-tier secrecy jurisdiction.** Trust assets in South Dakota more than quadrupled in a decade, reaching about $360 billion by the end of 2020 (the Guardian, using state data, put the figure at $367 billion, up from $75.5 billion in 2011). The state has no state income tax and no estate tax. It abolished the rule against perpetuities in 1983, so a trust can run forever, generation after generation, which is the entire point of a dynasty trust. Trust court filings are sealed. About 105 independent trust companies operate there, and a Governor's Task Force on Trust Administration, whose members come from the industry, drafts a favorable bill nearly every year. The Pandora Papers identified 206 US-based trusts holding more than $1 billion combined: 81 in South Dakota, 37 in Florida, 35 in Delaware, 24 in Texas, and 14 in Nevada. One documented South Dakota example: trusts for the family of a former vice president of the Dominican Republic whose sugar company stands accused of human rights and labor abuses, including bulldozing poor families' homes. Sources: https://www.icij.org/investigations/pandora-papers/us-trusts-offshore-south-dakota-tax-havens/ and https://listen.sdpb.org/politics/2021-10-04/south-dakota-lawmakers-spent-decades-creating-trust-industry-exposed-in-pandora-papers

**Delaware and Wyoming sell anonymous companies.** The Corporation Trust Center at 1209 North Orange Street in Wilmington, Delaware is the registered address of more than 285,000 companies (a figure from 2012 reporting, so the current count is a check item, not a settled number). Delaware has more than 2.1 million registered entities (2,157,482 in 2024) against about 1 million residents and does not publicly disclose LLC owners. Wyoming invented the LLC in 1977 and does not list shareholders publicly. In the best-known field experiment on shell company formation (Findley, Nielson, and Sharman, published as Global Shell Games), US formation agents were among the least likely in the world to demand identification from a customer, laxer than the classic island havens. That finding is the study's, reported here as such.

**The US refused the world's transparency deal and then ranked first in secrecy.** More than 100 jurisdictions, including the Cayman Islands and Luxembourg, now back the Common Reporting Standard, a 2014 agreement under which they swap bank account information with each other automatically. The United States refused to join. Its own law, FATCA, mostly runs one way: foreign banks report Americans' accounts to the IRS, while the US shares far less back. In the Tax Justice Network's Financial Secrecy Index 2022, the United States ranked number one, the worst rating recorded since the index began in 2009, with US secrecy supply up 31 percent since 2020 and nearly twice that of second-place Switzerland. Treasury Secretary Janet Yellen said it plainly: right now, the best place to hide and launder ill-gotten gains is actually the United States. Sources: https://thefactcoalition.org/the-u-s-ranked-most-secretive-financial-jurisdiction-in-the-world-just-the-facts-may-20/ and https://order.perssupport.nl/file/pressrelease/e72b06cd-ee3a-445a-8623-d191970826f0/f560c075-61cc-41ab-8f52-54aabb92366c/EMBARGOEDPressreleaseFinancialSecrecyIndex2022002.pdf

**Congress built an ownership registry, and then the registry was dismantled.** The Corporate Transparency Act passed on January 1, 2021, partly on leak-driven pressure, and ICIJ has noted the leaks helped bring it about. FinCEN's beneficial ownership reporting began on January 1, 2024. Then the reversal: Treasury suspended enforcement against US companies on March 2, 2025, FinCEN's interim final rule of March 26, 2025 exempted all US-formed companies, and a final rule dated August 11, 2026, effective August 14, 2026, made the domestic exemption permanent. FinCEN says it will delete the US-person ownership data already filed. The one reform the leaks produced in the US lasted about fourteen months. Sources: https://www.fincen.gov/boi and https://www.sidley.com/en/insights/newsupdates/2026/08/us-fincen-issues-final-rule-ending-beneficial-ownership-reporting-requirement

**The US Virgin Islands is the domestic offshore most people have never heard of.** Epstein ran his money through a US territory that sells a roughly 90 percent cut in qualifying taxes through its Economic Development Commission program, and in 2012 the territory created a lightly regulated class of offshore banks called international banking entities. Epstein got one of the first of those banking licenses. That story is told in full in Part 4 and in the Go Fish reconciliation, because it is where the American angle and the Epstein file become the same story.

## Part 3: The inequality mechanism, stated plainly

The chain from offshore secrecy to the poverty premium has four links. Each one is documented. None of them needs vibes.

**Link one: a large share of the world's wealth sits where tax authorities cannot see it.** Economist Gabriel Zucman's peer-reviewed estimate (Quarterly Journal of Economics, 2013) puts about 8 percent of global household financial wealth in tax havens, three-quarters of it unrecorded. His 2014 update put the total at about $7.6 trillion at the end of 2013, costing roughly $190 billion a year in lost tax revenue. US households hold about $1.2 trillion offshore. ICIJ's range for total offshore money runs from $5.6 trillion to $32 trillion, and a 2020 OECD study set a floor of $11.3 trillion. The Tax Justice Network's State of Tax Justice reports put the current annual loss at $480 billion a year (2023 edition: $311 billion to corporate tax abuse and $169 billion to offshore wealth) and $492 billion in the 2024 edition. Sources: https://gabriel-zucman.eu/files/Zucman2013QJE.pdf and https://www.icij.org/investigations/pandora-papers/global-investigation-tax-havens-offshore/?gclid=Cj0KCQjwwY-LBhD6ARIsACvT72OERpNRbXg95tv7Bce-66R5zZc4kiAhxbVRrl4SFN_q659Vf_9fQocaAlVeEALw_wcB

**Link two: the US tax base leaks at the top, and the IRS publishes the size of the hole.** The IRS projects a gross tax gap of $696 billion for tax year 2022 (released October 2024): $63 billion from nonfiling, $539 billion from underreporting, and $94 billion from underpayment. By tax type, individual income tax accounts for $514 billion of it. The net gap after projected enforcement is $606 billion. Underreporting is the biggest piece by far, and underreporting is the piece that requires something to hide behind. Source: https://fiscal.treasury.gov/system/files/2026-04/2025-Tax-Burden.pdf

**Link three: revenue that is not collected at the top is collected somewhere else, or the service just gets worse.** This is arithmetic, not ideology. A dollar of tax gap is a dollar that is either borrowed, cut from a service, or raised from someone whose income is reported by an employer and cannot be restructured through a trust in Sioux Falls. The poverty premium work in this project documents the other end of that pipe: the fees, markups, and penalty pricing that land on people with no slack. The EU Tax Observatory estimates multinationals shifted about $1 trillion of profit to low-tax jurisdictions in 2022, about 35 percent of profits booked abroad. FLAGGED: the Observatory's related estimate that billionaires pay an effective tax of about 0 to 0.5 percent of wealth per year reached this research through a secondary essay and should be checked against the Observatory's own report before use.

**Link four: hidden ownership sits inside the industries that charge the poor.** The cleanest documented loop in any of the troves is British, and it closes the circle in one story. The Paradise Papers showed that the Duchy of Lancaster, Queen Elizabeth II's private estate, held about £10 million in Cayman Islands and Bermuda funds. In 2005 it committed $7.5 million (about £5.7 million) to a Cayman fund called Dover Street VI, which invested in a private equity company that controlled BrightHouse, the UK rent-to-own retailer that sold appliances and furniture to cash-strapped customers on plans with interest rates as high as 99.9 percent. The Duchy said its indirect BrightHouse holding was worth £3,208, that its advisers chose the funds, and that it gained no tax advantage. BrightHouse was later forced by the UK finance watchdog to compensate customers. Read that loop slowly: money parked offshore earned returns from a company whose business model was charging poor people triple-digit rates for a washing machine. The offshore world and the poverty premium are not two systems. They are the supply side and the demand side of one system. Source: https://www.icij.org/investigations/paradise-papers/icij-releases-paradise-papers/

**The kleptocrat end of the pipe lands in American and British assets.** The Aliyev circle's 84 BVI companies and roughly $700 million of UK property (Part 1) are the documented example: oil-state money, moved through laundromat channels, parked in London real estate through companies whose owners were secret. Every house bought that way is a house priced by money that never had to answer questions, in a market where local buyers answer questions for a living. That is the mechanism. Secrecy is not a victimless paperwork choice. It is a subsidy, paid by everyone whose money is visible.

## Part 4: Epstein, the financial mechanics only

Epstein's crimes are documented in court records and are not the subject of this file. What fits an economic audit is his money machine, because his money machine was ordinary. It used a territorial tax program, two banks that failed at compliance, and fee rates that nobody checked for years. Every piece below is documented in court filings, settlements, or regulator releases.

**The two companies.** Financial Trust Company (set up in 1998, USVI tax benefits from 1999) and Southern Trust Company (set up in 2011, benefits from 2013) were his revenue businesses, both based in the US Virgin Islands and banked at JPMorgan. Together they reported more than $800 million in revenue from 1999 to 2018, and financial statements produced in litigation show Epstein collected at least $490 million in fees from them. Southern Trust described its work publicly as data mining and financial and biomedical informatics consulting. Reporting based on the court record puts Southern Trust's profit at about $300 million over six years at an effective tax rate of about 3.9 percent, under a territorial program built to cut qualifying taxes by about 90 percent.

**The tax breaks.** JPMorgan's own court filing in USVI v. JPMorgan put the two companies' USVI Economic Development benefits at roughly $300 million from 1999 to 2018 ($219.8 million for Financial Trust from 1999 to 2012, and $80.6 million for Southern Trust from 2013 to 2018). FLAGGED, in the honest sense: that $300 million figure is a litigant's estimate from a court filing, not a judicial accounting. The USVI attorney general alleged Southern Trust made fraudulent misrepresentations to the Economic Development Authority about its qualifications. The Epstein estate settled with the USVI in November 2022 for $105 million in cash, an agreement to repay more than $80 million in tax benefits, and half the proceeds of the sale of Little St. James island. Source: https://www.reuters.com/legal/jeffrey-epstein-estate-reaches-settlement-with-us-virgin-islands-2022-11-30/

**The documented client work.** Leon Black, co-founder of Apollo Global Management, is includable here for one reason: the financial facts are documented at the highest standard available. A review Apollo commissioned from the law firm Dechert, published in January 2021, found Black paid Epstein $158 million between 2012 and 2017 for tax and estate planning, and that the strategies saved him an estimated $1 billion, potentially more than $2 billion. Senate Finance Committee staff later traced $170 million (March 2025). Senator Wyden's committee work noted that Black paid the law firm Paul Weiss about $2 million in 2013 and 2014 for comparable estate and family office work, which makes Epstein's rates roughly 30 times higher, a gap Wyden called inexplicable by any standard measure. Black stepped down as Apollo's CEO in March 2021. In 2023 he paid the USVI $62.5 million to settle potential claims arising from the territory's Epstein investigation. He has denied wrongdoing and any involvement in Epstein's crimes. Source: https://www.finance.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/wyden_wall_street_epstein_report.pdf

**JPMorgan.** Epstein's bank from 1998 to 2013, which kept him for five years after his 2008 conviction, paid $290 million to settle the victims' class action (Doe 1 v. JPMorgan Chase, SDNY, agreed June 12, 2023, final approval November 9, 2023. The judgment states it is not an admission of liability) and $75 million to the USVI in September 2023, split as $30 million for charitable organizations, $25 million to strengthen anti-trafficking law enforcement, and $20 million in attorneys' fees. Sources: https://www.reuters.com/legal/us-judge-weighs-jpmorgans-290-million-settlement-with-epstein-accusers-2023-11-09/ and https://www.reuters.com/world/us/jpmorgan-settles-epstein-lawsuit-with-us-virgin-islands-75-mln-2023-09-26/ and https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/new-york/nysdce/1:2022cv10019/590048/284/

**Deutsche Bank.** His bank from 2013 to 2018, holding roughly 40 accounts for him. The New York Department of Financial Services consent order of July 2020 imposed a $150 million penalty covering three matters (Epstein, Danske Bank Estonia, and FBME), so that figure must never be totaled as an Epstein-only penalty. DFS found the bank inexcusably failed to detect or prevent millions of dollars of suspicious transactions, including more than $800,000 in suspicious cash withdrawals over about four years and more than $7 million in settlement payments. Deutsche separately paid $75 million to settle victims' claims in May 2023. Sources: https://www.dfs.ny.gov/reports_and_publications/press_releases/pr202007071 and https://www.reuters.com/legal/deutsche-bank-pay-75-mln-settle-jeffrey-epstein-accusers-suit-wsj-2023-05-18/

**Where the money ended up.** The Epstein Victims' Compensation Program awarded nearly $125 million to about 150 eligible survivors, and more than $121 million was paid out to the roughly 136 who accepted their offers. The estate's own arc is fully documented in probate and is covered in the Go Fish reconciliation below. The short version: the fortune was not hidden billions. It was about $578 million on paper at death, it came overwhelmingly from fees and territorial tax treatment, and the paper trail is the story.

## Part 5: Reconciling the Operation Go Fish leads

Victoria and Atlas ran the Operation Go Fish investigation in October 2026 and documented six Epstein leads (family newsletter, October 25, 2026). Each lead is reconciled below against the public record gathered for this cross-reference. Verdicts use three words only: CONFIRMED, STILL OPEN, or CONTRADICTED. None of the six leads is contradicted.

**Lead 1: Epstein was director and chairman of offshore Liquid Funding Ltd. in the Paradise Papers, with Appleby as registered agent, and the company dissolved in 2015. Verdict: CONFIRMED.**
ICIJ's Offshore Leaks Database, built from the Appleby files, lists Liquid Funding, Ltd. as a Bermuda company incorporated on October 19, 2000, and lists Jeffrey E. Epstein as its director from November 9, 2001 to March 30, 2007 and its chairman from November 9, 2001 to March 19, 2007. Bear Stearns disclosed an approximate 40 percent equity interest in it in its fiscal 2002 Form 10-K filed with the SEC. FLAGGED: secondary accounts say Bear Stearns set the vehicle up, but the primary sources located confirm the registration and the 40 percent stake, not who created it. The vehicle was a structured investment conduit whose outstanding liabilities reached roughly $6.7 billion in 2006 (Fitch). Shareholders voted for a members' voluntary liquidation on November 25, 2015, which matches the Go Fish dissolution date. One sharpening: ICIJ lists Liquid Funding Holdings, LLC as a shareholder, with no percentage given in the database, so the structure was Bermuda on top with an American shareholder underneath, which is the whole cross-reference in miniature. Sources: https://offshoreleaks.icij.org/nodes/82004676 and https://offshoreleaks.icij.org/nodes/80063035

**Lead 2: $80 million in Financial Trust Company investments was transferred to Jeepers, Inc. and later disputed, and the Fortress investment trail is the open thread. Verdict: CONFIRMED as to the investment, the transfer, and the dispute. STILL OPEN as to a combined total across both settlement streams.**
The paper trail sits in a federal court complaint preserved in the DOJ document release: Jeepers, Inc. v. D.B. Zwirn Partners, LLC and Daniel Zwirn, filed in the US District Court for the US Virgin Islands. The complaint states that Financial Trust Company invested a total of $80 million in the D.B. Zwirn Special Opportunities Fund between 2002 and January 1, 2005 ($50 million across three tranches in 2002, $10 million more on June 1, 2003, and a final $20 million on January 1, 2005), and that Jeepers, Inc., a USVI corporation based at 6100 Red Hook Quarter, Suite B-3, St. Thomas, is the assignee of all of Financial Trust Company's rights in the fund. The complaint alleges the fund's managers concealed an accounting fraud, and it notes that Zwirn announced an SEC investigation in 2007 into improper transfers and overcharging.
