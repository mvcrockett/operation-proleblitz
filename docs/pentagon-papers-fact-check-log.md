### Item 10, Lack of Preventative Coverage: CORRECTION

Claims checked: 6. Verified 5, corrected 1.

Claim: the dollar total in the CDC figure that chronic and mental health conditions account for 90 percent of national healthcare spending.

### Item 21, Rent Inflation Without Wage Growth: CORRECTION and FLAG

Claims checked: 7. Verified 5, corrected 1, flagged 1.

Correction claim: the housing wage in the National Low Income Housing Coalition's Out of Reach report.

Before: "The National Low Income Housing Coalition's Out of Reach report calculates that a full-time worker needs to earn about $32 an hour to afford a modest two-bedroom apartment at fair market rent without being cost-burdened."

After: "The National Low Income Housing Coalition's Out of Reach report calculates that a full-time worker needs to earn $33.63 an hour to afford a modest two-bedroom apartment at fair market rent without being cost-burdened."

Source: National Low Income Housing Coalition, Out of Reach 2025. https://nlihc.org/resource/now-available-out-reach-2025-high-cost-housing

Flag claim: "Median gross rent has climbed roughly 70 percent since 2000, while typical wages, adjusted for inflation, grew by less than a fifth over the same period."

Why it is flagged: the text names no source for the 70 percent figure, and the published series do not produce it on one consistent basis. Census figures put nominal median gross rent at $602 in 2000 and $1,487 in 2024, a rise of about 147 percent. An analysis of Bureau of Labor Statistics and Census series puts nominal rents up about 146 percent since 2000 with consumer prices up about 96 percent, which makes the real rise roughly a quarter rather than 70 percent. The sentence also sets what looks like a nominal rent figure against wage growth stated in real terms. The claim stays in the text and is flagged rather than rewritten, because no single sourced replacement covers both halves of the sentence.

Source consulted: "Have Wages Kept Up with Housing Costs?" https://www.realab.blog/p/have-wages-kept-up-with-housing-costs
