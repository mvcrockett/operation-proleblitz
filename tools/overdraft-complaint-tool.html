Step 2. Your complaint packet

A. Dispute and refund letter to your bank

Where to file it: Send this to your bank first. You can hand it in at a branch, send it through the secure message center in your banking app, or mail it to the address on your statement. Keep a copy and write down the date you sent it. Under Regulation E, once a bank gets an error notice it generally has 10 business days to investigate or put the money back while it looks into things. If the bank says no, the two complaints below are your next step, and having this letter proves you asked.

B. Complaint to the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau

Where to file it: Go to consumerfinance.gov and choose File a complaint, or call 1-855-411-2372. Paste the text above into the box that asks what happened. The Bureau sends your complaint to the bank, and the bank has to answer, usually within 15 days. Banks take these seriously because every answer is read by a regulator.

C. Complaint to your state Attorney General

Where to file it: Search for your state Attorney General consumer complaint page, or call their consumer protection hotline and ask where to send it. Most states take complaints online and by mail. The Attorney General may not take your case one by one, but your complaint goes into the pattern file. When enough people complain about the same bank, that file is what investigations are built on.

One more thing worth knowing. If your bank is a national bank and it will not budge, you can also complain to the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency. If it is a credit union, the National Credit Union Administration takes complaints. You do not have to pick just one office. Filing in more than one place is allowed, and it is often how people finally get their money back.
